\section*{INTRODUCTION}

This assignment is all about maximum likelihood parameter estimation (i.e.) we take three 2D Gaussian classes and estimate their parameters, $\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k$. Not only do we estimate the parameters, we also split the data into train and test sets. We use the test set to check the classifier built from the estimated parameters and observe the confusion matrix, accuracy, and decision boundary. We do this for isotropic, diagonal, and full covariance matrices, with both shared and different covariances across classes.

\section*{PARAMETER ESTIMATION AND CLASSIFICATION}
\[
\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k=\frac{1}{N_k}\sum_i\mathbf{x}_{ki},\qquad
\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k=\frac{1}{N_k}\sum_i
(\mathbf{x}_{ki}-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k)
(\mathbf{x}_{ki}-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
\[
g_k(\mathbf{x})=-\tfrac12(\mathbf{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k)^{\mathsf T}
\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k)
-\tfrac12\log|\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k|.
\]

Here $\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k$ is the estimated mean and $\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k$ is the estimated covariance matrix. The function $g_k(\mathbf{x})$ gives us log-density score. Since, the priors are equal here it tells you given the parameters how likely it belongs to class-k.

The confusion matrices use true classes as rows and predicted classes as columns.

\textbf{Taking a closer look into the decision boundaries:}\par\medskip

Let $A_k=\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k^{-1}$. Then the squared Mahalanobis distance expands as
\[
(\mathbf{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k)^{\mathsf T}A_k
(\mathbf{x}-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k)
=\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}A_k\mathbf{x}
-2\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k^{\mathsf T}A_k\mathbf{x}
+\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k^{\mathsf T}A_k\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k.
\]
Substituting this into $g_k(\mathbf{x})$ gives
\[
g_k(\mathbf{x})=-\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}A_k\mathbf{x}
+\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k^{\mathsf T}A_k\mathbf{x}
-\tfrac12\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k^{\mathsf T}A_k\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_k
-\tfrac12\log|\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_k|.
\]
When we compare two classes $i$ and $j$, their scores differ by
\[
g_i(\mathbf{x})-g_j(\mathbf{x})
=-\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}(A_i-A_j)\mathbf{x}
+(\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_i^{\mathsf T}A_i
-\hat{\boldsymbol\mu}_j^{\mathsf T}A_j)\mathbf{x}
+\text{terms independent of }\mathbf{x}.
\]
When the classes share the same covariance, $A_i=A_j$. The quadratic term $\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}(A_i-A_j)\mathbf{x}$ cancels and leaves a linear decision boundary. When the classes have different covariances, that term generally remains and creates a nonlinear decision boundary.
